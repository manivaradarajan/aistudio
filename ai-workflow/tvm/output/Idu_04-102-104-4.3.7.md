**Verse Translation**

O beautiful Vamana, who stretched forth Your celebrated feet to claim the earth!
O wondrous Lord, who stands near for those who fold their hands to Your acclaimed feet, so they may attain You!
Though I cannot worship You with fragrant flowers and water,
Your sacred, luminous form, which captivates all speech, rests upon my very soul.

**ஆ – (6000 Commentary)**

He says: You descended to protect surrendered souls, performing wondrous, divine deeds. It is not merely that if I were to perform some service, offering exceedingly cool, sacred, and fragrant flowers and water at Your holy feet—which are attainable only through devotion—that service would become an object of enjoyment for You. Rather, even though I perform no service at all, for You, whose divine form of radiant light is immeasurable by speech or mind, my very existence has become Your sustainer and object of enjoyment.

**ஒ - (9000 Commentary)**

In the seventh verse, the Alvar expresses his amazement, saying: “Even when I lack both love and the actions befitting that love, my very existence has become the very cause of Your life! What an extraordinary affection You possess!”

[Starting with *kuraikaḻalkaḷ*...] *Kuraikaḻalkaḷ* refers to His large, holy feet; it can also mean resounding.
[Starting with *kuraikkaḻal*...] O wondrous One, who stands in such a way that even those who show just a slight inclination toward You may attain You!
[Starting with *virai*, etc.] Though I am unable to perform service with fine offerings, for Your sacred form of light, which is beyond the realm of speech, my very existence has become the sustainer. What a wonder this is!

**ப – (12000 Commentary)**

Next, the Alvar says: Even though I am utterly incapable of rendering any service, Your form, whose very nature is to claim me for service by making me exclusively Yours, is making my soul its object.

*Kurai kaḻalkaḷ* – The holy feet adorned with the heroic anklets (worn to proclaim that He makes souls exclusively His). *nīṭṭi* – stretching them forth. *maṇ koṇṭa* – and taking the world. *kōla vāmaṉā* – O Vamana of beautiful form!
*Kuraikaḻal kaikūppuvārkaḷ* – for those who perform a single gesture of folded hands toward those holy feet. *kūṭa niṉṟa* – You who stood (as both means and end) so they could attain You. *māyaṉē* – O wondrous One!
*Viraikoḷ pūvum nīrum koṇṭu* – Taking fragrant flowers and water (for washing feet, etc.). *ēttamāṭṭēṉēlum* – though I am incapable of offering praise (as an act of worship toward You),
*uṉ urai koḷ cōtit tiru uruvam* – Your sacred form of radiance, which is *urai koḷ* – immeasurable by the words of the Vedanta, *eṉṉatu āvimēlatu* – is intently focused upon my soul.

**இ - (24000 Commentary)**

In the seventh verse, he says: “Even though I lack the love mentioned before and the actions befitting it, my very existence becomes the cause of Your life!”

[*Kuraikaḻalkaḷ*] This can mean “possessing great enjoyability” or “the sound of the ornaments.”

[*Nīṭṭi maṇ koṇṭa*] As stated, “encompassing the entire earth,” the very feet that were the *means* by which He brought the whole earth under His control became the *end* for the Alvar.

[*Māyaṉē*] He exclaims, “That You can be attained with just a single folding of hands! What a wonder this is!”

[*Virai*, etc.] “If You are such that You can be attained with just a single folding of hands, then it is only proper for a conscious soul to perform service with offerings like flowers. And yet, I was not able to do so.” Even though I could not perform the prescribed service with fragrant flowers and water, as described in the verse, “Let them sprinkle the entire earth with ice-cold water” (Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 6.126.31 [?]).

[*Uṉ uraikoḷ cōtit tiru uruvam*] *Uraikoḷ* means the form is such that one can only stand with a parched mouth, repeating, “That one! That one!” For they say, “When Rama was ruling the kingdom, the people’s conversations were only ‘Rama, Rama, Rama,’ and the whole world became filled with Rama” (Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 6.128.105 [?]). Alternatively, it means the sacred form that is beyond the object of speech. Or, it means the sacred form that is beyond comparison or assay, as it is said, “Even the finest gold, tested and described, cannot compare to the radiance of Your sacred form” (Irantam Tiruvantati 1).

[*Eṉṉatu āvimēlatē*] Your sacred form, so unique, is upon my very existence. How is it that my own existence has become the sustainer and enjoyment for the form relished by the eternal celestial beings! It is just like the saying, “The mind of a hungry person is fixed upon his rice.” For the Alvar himself is one who exists saying, “I am food, I am food, I am food” (Taittiriya Upanishad, Bhriguvalli 3.10.6).

**ஈடு: – (36000 Commentary)**

**Seventh Verse**

The Alvar says: Even when I am without the love mentioned before and without the actions befitting that love, my very existence has become the cause of Your life!

*Stretching forth Your celebrated feet:* As described in the phrase, “He who stood encompassing the entire earth” (Tirunetuntantakam 5), He stretched forth His holy feet to bring the whole expanse of the world within their embrace. The word *kurai* signifies both expanse and sound.

*O beautiful Vamana who claimed the earth!* Even after He had claimed the earth, the beautiful Vamana form remained brimming in the Alvar’s heart. He understands that the Lord took that form not just to restore the kingdom to Indra, but so that he, the Alvar, could experience Him. The previous line spoke of the Lord's supreme reach; this line speaks of how He is attainable by a mere folding of hands.

*For those who fold their hands to Your celebrated feet:* This can be understood in two ways:
1. Those who folded their hands in reverence at Your holy feet stood ready to attain You.
2. O wondrous One, for those who fold their hands to You, Your celebrated feet stood ready to be attained.
Ultimately, His holy feet are both the goal of the folded hands and the means by which He is attained through them.

Just as the kingdom was apportioned to Indra, the beauty of those holy feet was apportioned to the Alvar. What was the *means* for Indra became the *end* for the Alvar.

*O wondrous One!* He exclaims, “That You can be attained with just a single folding of hands! What a wonder this is!” It is truly a wonder that the Lord, who is the object of enjoyment for the eternal celestial beings, is attainable by a mere gesture of reverence.

*Though I cannot worship You with fragrant flowers and water:* If You are so easily accessible, attainable by a mere folding of hands, then the proper thing for a soul to do is to perform service with offerings like flowers. Yet, I have not done that—worship with fragrant water and fragrant flowers, as described in the verse: “Let them sprinkle the entire earth with ice-cold water” (Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 6.126.31 [?]). I have not been able to perform such prescribed service.

*Your sacred, luminous form which captivates all speech:* The phrase "captivates speech" has three meanings:
1. It describes a form so beautiful that, out of a desperate desire to see it, one can only stand with a parched mouth, repeating, “That one! That one!” This is how people were during Rama’s reign, when “the people’s conversations were only ‘Rama, Rama, Rama,’ and the whole world became filled with Rama” (Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 6.128.105 [?]).
2. It can also mean that His sacred form is beyond the capacity of speech to describe.
3. It can mean a sacred form that is beyond comparison or assay, like gold so pure it needs no touchstone, as stated in the verse, “Even the finest gold, tested and described, cannot compare to the radiance of Your sacred form” (Irantam Tiruvantati 1).

*Rests upon my very soul:* Your sacred form rests upon my existence. How is it that my own life-essence has become the sustainer and enjoyment for the divine form relished by the eternal celestial beings! It is just like the saying, “The mind of a hungry person is fixed upon his rice.” For the Alvar himself is one who exists declaring, “I am food, I am food, I am food” (Taittiriya Upanishad, Bhriguvalli 3.10.6), offering himself as sustenance for the Lord.
