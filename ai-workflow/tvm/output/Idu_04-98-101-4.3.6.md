### Tiruvaymoli 4.3.6

**Verse Translation**

O You who bear in Your beautiful hand the discus of time and the white conch!
O Narayana, who swallowed and then brought forth the entire world!
Though I cry out in a great lament, calling to You again and again,
even if You do not come at all, Your lotus-like, resounding ankleted feet are the perfect ornament for my head.

**ஆ – (6000 Commentary):**

The Alvar says: “You have united with me in a way that is indescribable even for You, the omniscient and omnipotent Lord.” Alternatively, the meaning is: “Even if You—who are adorned with divine weapons like the conch and discus, which have the nature of consuming the enemies of Your devotees, and who are distinguished by auspicious qualities like compassion, love, and approachability—do not unite with me in this way, please make me such that Your very existence is the ultimate delight for me.”

**ஒ - (9000 Commentary):**

In the sixth verse, the Alvar declares: "Even if You, who are so filled with love, do not unite with me, Your very existence is my sole sustenance and everything else." Why does he speak of his own love? It is because the Lord’s own overwhelming love has, in turn, transformed him into a loving devotee.

[O You who bear the discus of time…] He calls out, describing His beauty: “O You who hold in Your beautiful hand the sacred discus, which is the very embodiment of enemy destruction, and the sacred conch, whose whiteness is a striking contrast to Your divine dark hue!” He continues, speaking of His protective nature: “O You who are so deeply affectionate toward the worlds, swallowing them whole and then bringing them forth, performing the appropriate acts of protection for them at every turn!” Even if there is no occasion for You to come when I cry out and call to You in this way, [the perfect ornament…] Your desirable sacred feet will still be my delight.

**ப – (12000 Commentary):**

Next, the Alvar expresses the deep love he feels for the Lord, who is Himself a great lover, saying: “O Protector, equipped to destroy the enemies of Your devotees! Even if You do not show Your face in response to my plea, the connection to Your sacred feet is the very foundation of my existence.”

**You who bear** in Your **beautiful hand**—a hand that enhances their beauty—the **discus** whose nature is to be like **death** (`kālaṉ`) to Your enemies, **along with** the **white conch** (which delights Your devotees); and yet, despite being so equipped for protection, You are the one **who swallowed** the entire **world** (the object of protection), leaving nothing behind, to save it from the peril of the cosmic dissolution by keeping it in Your stomach, and then **brought it forth** (so it could be seen again once the danger had passed). **O Narayana**, who possesses this unconditional bond! Calling out **“Narayana!” again and again**, proclaiming these qualities one by one, **I**, who am unworthy to have You rise from Your place and come to me, **cry out** in a great lament and **call** to You. **Even if You do not come** in a way that I can perceive **any sign** (as expressed in, “We cannot discern Your proximity or trace”), for **my head**—which possesses an existence that cannot endure without You (as described in, “My love for You swells ever greater”)—**Your sacred feet (`kazaṟ`)**, which are **like a lotus** in their unassuming beauty and wear heroic **anklets (`kurai`) that resound** with the fame of Your protection of devotees, **are (`ām`)** a glorious **ornament (`kōlam`)** and a radiant light. The implied meaning is: my love for Your sacred feet is inseparable from my very existence.

**இ - (24000 Commentary):**

In the sixth verse, the Alvar says: “Even if You, who are so full of love, do not come near me, Your very existence is my sustenance and everything else.” Just as my existence is sustenance for You even when I am unable to serve You, Your existence is sustenance for me even when You do not come to me. Why does the Alvar speak of his own love in the midst of describing the Lord’s? It is to show that the Lord’s overwhelming love has transformed him, too, into a loving devotee.

[O You who bear the discus of time…] You hold the sacred discus, which is death to Your adversaries, along with the sacred conch, whose whiteness is a beautiful contrast to Your divine hue, in a hand that makes even them appear as ornaments. Whiteness is the defining quality of the conch, just as the destruction of adversaries is the defining quality of the discus.

[O You who swallowed and then brought forth the entire world!] During the great dissolution, You protected the world without exception by keeping it in Your sacred stomach, and at the time of creation, You brought it forth for all to see.

[O Narayana!] This name signifies the inherent relationship that is the basis for Your protecting all without exception and for doing so without expecting anything in return.

[Again and again] He calls out, speaking of the beauty of His hand holding the weapons, of His nature as a friend in times of distress, and of the inherent bond that makes Him inseparable even when those other qualities are not manifest. The repetition shows how deeply he is immersed in each of these aspects.

[I cry out in a great lament and call] When I, who am utterly powerless, cry out, my voice should be so great as to unsettle You from Your very place and make You come to show Your face. When I call out, “Come, let me see You!” [even if You do not come at all]—even if there is no indication whatsoever of Your arrival, as when it was said of Krishna, “He is staying in Kusasthala and will arrive here tomorrow morning,” showing that he had already set out—[the perfect ornament for my head…] Your feet, which are both my rightful goal and my supreme delight, are the greatest joy for my head, which longs for them, as declared: “I shall bear them on my head; only then will I find peace.” `Kurai` means expanse, signifying the excellence of their enjoyability, or it can mean the sound of His anklets.

Why does he say, “Even if You do not come, they are an ornament”? He means: “It is not right that You should fail to come. But even if that impossibility occurs, this is my resolve.” The deeper meaning is: “You are the one who made me this way.”

**ஈடு: – (36000 Commentary):**

In the sixth verse, the Alvar declares: "Even if You, who are so filled with love, do not come near me, Your very existence is my sustenance and everything else." He is saying, "Just as my existence is Your sustenance and delight even when I am unable to be of service to You, Your existence is my sustenance and delight even when You do not come to me."

While he is speaking of the Lord's loving nature, why does he suddenly speak of his own? When two sentient beings unite and interact, the sublime experiences that arise are felt on both sides. Until now, he has been speaking of the Lord’s loving devotion. Now, he speaks of his own to show that the Lord’s overwhelming devotion has, in turn, transformed him into a loving devotee as well.

[O You who bear the discus of time and the white conch in Your hand] This, then, is the way he calls out. "O You who bear the sacred discus, which is death itself to Your enemies, along with the sacred conch, whose whiteness is a beautiful contrast to Your dark, raincloud-hued body! You hold them in Your beautiful hand—a hand so lovely it requires a ritual waving of lights to ward off the evil eye, even when unadorned—as gently as if You were bearing a flower." Just as whiteness is the defining quality of the sacred conch, so too is the destruction of adversaries the defining quality of the sacred discus.

[O You who swallowed and then brought forth the entire world!] He does not merely adorn Himself with these weapons and remain perfectly composed while His creation suffers; He is a true friend in times of distress. During the great peril of the cosmic dissolution, He protected the world without exception by keeping it in His sacred stomach, and once that danger passed, He brought it forth for all to see. This is how unconditionally He protects.

[O Narayana!] This name reveals the inherent relationship that is the reason He protects so unconditionally, and it shows why He does not need to feel anguish, searching for some reciprocation and thinking, “He protected us.” Does anyone lament, thinking, ‘My mother protected me in her womb, what can I ever do for her in return?’ The inherent relationship is the basis for this, and it is the same with Him. Indeed, He is the one who, just as a mother does, kept you in His own stomach and granted you salvation.

[Again and again] He calls out, meditating on the beauty of His hand holding the divine weapons, on His nature as a friend in times of distress suited to every occasion, and on the inherent bond that makes Him impossible to forsake even if these other qualities were absent. The repetition shows how deeply he is immersed in each one of these truths.

[I cry out in a great lament and call] When I, who am so powerless, cry out and call to You—when I raise a great cry, a voice so powerful it should unsettle You even in Your supreme abode and compel You to come—[even if You do not come at all]—even if there is no indication whatsoever of Your arrival, unlike when it was said of Krishna, “He is staying in Kusasthala and will arrive here tomorrow morning,” which showed He had already departed from there and was on his way here. For a devotee, even the sweet sound of the horn proclaiming the Lord’s name is a sign of His arrival, but here, there is nothing.

[The perfect ornament for my head] For my head, which remains ever bowed in anticipation, as Vibhishana declared, “I shall bear them on my head; only then will I find peace” (Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 17.16).

[Your lotus-like] Like those who long to adorn themselves with a freshly bloomed flower.

[Resounding ankleted feet] The word `kurai` can mean ‘expanse,’ thus signifying the excellence of their enjoyability. Alternatively, it can refer to the sound (`dhvani`) of His heroic anklets.

Why does he say, “Even if You do not come, they are an ornament”? He means: “It is not right that You should fail to come. But even if that which should not happen, happens, this is my resolve.” The deeper meaning is a loving complaint: “After all, You are the one who made me this way at the very foundation.” [Translator's Note: The word `aṭi` means both "foundation/beginning" and "feet," creating a poignant double meaning.] If he were to let go of those feet, he would simply declare, as he does elsewhere, “I have no one but You!” (Tiruvaymoli 6.10.10).
