**Verse 4.9.4**

Intending to destroy, the deceitful demoness offered her breast to Your mouth.
You destroyed her life, O wondrous cowherd! O Vamana! O Madhava!
Though I failed to worship You with cool, blossomed garlands at those crucial moments,
the very garland adorning Your cool, flower-wreathed, towering crown is now my life itself.

**ஆ – (6000 Commentary):**

The Alvar says: "Though I did not worship You—who by nature destroy the enemies of Your devotees, who grant the desires of Your devotees, and who are the Lord of Sri—with cool, blossomed garlands at the appropriate times, my very soul has become the adorning garland for Your cool, flower-wreathed, towering crown."

**ஒ - (9000 Commentary):**

In the fourth verse, the Alvar expresses his joy, saying: "The service that should have been rendered to relieve the fatigue from Your deeds, like drinking milk from Pūtanā’s breast, and to honor Your exquisite tenderness as the Lord of Sri—my very life has become that service!"

[Beginning with "cool, blossomed garlands..."] "Even though I was unable to be present at the proper times with cool flower garlands to perform service for You, the garland placed upon Your sacred crown—a crown that cannot be without cool, sacred garlands—has become my very life!"

**ப – (12000 Commentary):**

Next, the Alvar says: "You are the Lord of Sri who destroyed Pūtanā, who appeared in a favorable guise, and thereby made me Your own, who am unworthy. How is it that my very life has now become a special adornment for Your sacred crown!"

To destroy (மாய்த்தல்), intending (எண்ணி) to do so, she placed (தந்த) her breast in His sacred coral mouth (வாய்). She was a deceitful (மாயம்) demoness (பேய்) who had assumed the guise of a mother, favorable to Him. You who destroyed (மாய்த்த) the life (உயிர்) of that demoness! O wondrous one (மாயனே), peerless in Your role as a young cowherd (ஆயன்)! O Vamana (வாமனனே), who by this great deed made Your incarnation as Vamana a success, an incarnation that reveals Your lordship, making the world exclusively Yours, as declared in the scriptures! O Madhava (மாதவா), who thereby also secured Your inherent nature as the Lord of Sri! Even though (ஏலும்) I failed to worship (வணங்கு) You (உன்னை)—You who are the vanquisher of such enemies in favorable guise—at the appropriate times (போது) with garlands (மாலை கொண்டு) of unique blossoms (பூ), cool (தண்) to the touch, my very life (எனது உயிரே) has become the garland (கண்ணி) that is lovingly placed (புனையும்) as an adornment (அலங்காரமாக) upon Your sacred crown (முடிக்கு)—a crown that is towering (நெடு), indicating Your supreme sovereignty, and adorned with garlands (மாலை) that are made of flowers (பூ), cool (தண்), and lovely.

**இ - (24000 Commentary):**

In the fourth verse, the Alvar says: "At the time You destroyed Pūtanā and others, I was not there to relieve Your fatigue with loving service. Even so, for Your tender divine form, which cannot endure without such service, my very life has become that loving service!"

[Intending to destroy] She set out to bring about the dissolution of the entire universe. If the soul of a thing is harmed, does its form have any existence? If the vital spot is struck, there is no need to harm each limb. As the great king Sugriva said, "She has come to destroy us, without a doubt" (Ramayana 4.11.42 [?]). [She gave her breast to Your mouth] The Alvar feels that she placed the breast in his own sacred coral mouth, so he says, "she gave." Or, since "giving" and "placing" can be synonyms, it may simply mean "she placed." [Deceitful demoness] Beyond the ignorance that came with her birth, she came intent on deception. That is, she did not appear as a demoness but as a mother. [You destroyed her life] He contained the cosmic destruction she intended within her single self. He drank her milk as a mere child, and she perished simply because of His intrinsic nature, which is poison to the demonic race. [O wondrous cowherd] O cowherd endowed with unimaginable powers! [O Vamana] In that incarnation as Krishna, when Pūtanā gave her breast, at least there was a mother to give Him ambrosia from her own breast as an antidote. But in this incarnation as Vamana, there was not even that. His mother and father were preoccupied with their penances, and on the very day of His birth, He went to where the demons were. I was not there to help Him even in that state. [O Madhava] When the divine couple is together in all their splendor, one is compelled to offer prayers for their well-being, thinking, "What might befall this beautiful union?" Indeed, Their attendants are ever-singing, "Countless years to the Goddess who lives in splendor as Your very form on Your right chest!" (Tiruppallandu 2). [With cool, blossomed garlands] With cool flower garlands, at those very moments—when Pūtanā approached, when He went to where the demons were, or when He was with the Divine Mother—though I did not worship Him; though I was not able to fall at His sacred feet and perform loving service to relieve His fatigue. [For Your cool, flower-wreathed, towering crown] The garland I long to place upon Your sacred crown—a crown eternally adorned with cool flower garlands—has become my very life! "Life" here means existence itself.

**ஈடு: – (36000 Commentary):**

In the fourth verse, the Alvar says: "At the time You destroyed Pūtanā and others, I was not there to relieve Your fatigue with loving service. Even so, for Your tender divine form, which cannot endure without such service, the garland placed upon it has become my very existence!"

[Intending to destroy] The way she came, intent on her mission, it was certain that some would have to perish. By not specifying *whom* she intended to destroy, the Alvar implies: "Indeed, she set out to bring about the dissolution of the entire universe." After all, if the soul is harmed, the body perishes on its own. If the life-center is struck, there is no need to strike each limb individually. And is He not the one life for all the worlds? As the great king Sugriva said, "She has come to destroy us, without a doubt" (Ramayana 4.11.42 [?]). [She gave her breast to His mouth] The Alvar feels the poison placed in the Lord’s sacred coral mouth as if it were placed in his own, and so he says, "she gave." Or, it may be that the words for "giving" and "placing" are synonymous, and so it simply means "she placed." [Deceitful demoness] Beyond the ignorance that came with her birth, she came intent on deception. That is, she did not come as a demoness, but as a mother. As the scriptures say, "the demoness who came as a mother" (Tirumoli 1.5.6). [You destroyed her life] He contained the destruction she had intended within her own self. [O wondrous cowherd] O cowherd endowed with astonishing power! For He drank her milk as a mere infant, and she offered it in the guise of a mother; she perished simply due to His intrinsic nature.

[O Vamana] In that incarnation as Krishna, when Pūtanā offered her breast, at least there was a mother there to offer her own milk as an antidote. As it is said, "To feed the one who had drunk from the fierce breast, the cowherd mother drew near and placed her own breast in His mouth" (Mundram Tiruvantati 74), and "Lifting up the great Lord who had drunk the demoness’s milk, the cowherd mother fearlessly gave Him her breast" (Mundram Tiruvantati 29). But in the Vamana incarnation, even this was absent. His mother and father, after giving birth to Him, were preoccupied with their penance, leaving Him to go all by Himself to the very place where the demonic beings resided. For Him to venture into the enemy's territory is not at all like them coming to where He was; it is a cause for extreme anguish. This is the very sentiment captured in the verse: "O great benefactor, what if You had not been born with splendor and raised with care, but had instead taken the name Vamana, so small!" (Periya Tiruvantati 16), where the poet clutches his stomach in empathetic pain.

[O Madhava] As the saying goes, "There are no evil days for those who possess Sri." The reason He escaped these perils is that Sri was watching over Him from her place on His chest. Even if there were no danger at all, the sight of the great Divine Mother and the Lord together is enough to make devotees feel an urgent need to offer prayers for their well-being. Indeed, Their attendants are always singing, "Countless years to the Goddess who lives in splendor as Your very form on Your right chest!" (Tiruppallandu 2).

[With cool, blossomed garlands] With cool flower garlands, at those very moments when Pūtanā and others approached, I was not able to fall at Your sacred feet and perform loving service to relieve Your fatigue. [For Your cool, flower-wreathed, towering crown] The garland lovingly placed upon Your sacred crown—a crown that cannot be without cool flower garlands and which signals Your supreme sovereignty—my life, my very existence, has become that garland.
