**Verse 3**

You are the One Form, the two forms, the three forms, the many forms;
You become the five elements, the two luminaries, and the formless Spirit.
O Narayana, who mounted the serpent and slumbered in the middle of the ocean!
Embracing Your entire being within my heart, my soul’s anguish has been destroyed.

**ஆ – (6000 Commentary):**

He is distinguished by a divine form that is non-material, eternal, and perfect. As the Lord of the supreme abode, Sri Vaikuntha, He is the creator of unmanifest primal matter, the great principle of intellect, the threefold principle of "I-ness," the mind and other faculties, the five elements, the sun, the moon, and all other entities, as well as the host of sentient souls dwelling within them. For the sake of their creation, He abides within them as their inner Self. For the sake of protecting the created cosmos, He reclines on the serpent couch in the middle of the Milk Ocean. The Alvar says: "O Narayana! Experiencing Your magnificent quality of accepting the acts of remembrance, praise, and other services I perform for You as all the enjoyments Your divine form could ever desire, I too have been cured of my afflictions."

**ஒ – (9000 Commentary):**

In the third verse, the Alvar describes the Lord’s profound sense of fulfillment, which arises from His loving relationship with him.

[Beginning with "You are the One Form..."] Before creation, You possess a unique, transcendent form entirely Your own. You created unmanifest primal matter, the great principle of intellect, the threefold principle of "I-ness," the faculties of the mind, the five elements, and the sun, moon, and other celestial bodies. To sustain them, You became their very soul. [Beginning with "who mounted the serpent..."] So that the beings created by You would have someone to take refuge in, You gracefully recline on the divine serpent Adisesa in the middle of the Milk Ocean, O Lord whose very nature is to bestow grace upon all creatures! Taking all the sustaining, nourishing, and beautifying elements required for Your divine form as aids for *my* devotion, the anguish in Your own heart has at last been destroyed. Alternatively, the final line [“my soul’s anguish has been destroyed”] can also mean, “I have become free from sorrow.”

**ப – (12000 Commentary):**

The Alvar declares, “By experiencing Your divine form in this way, my soul, which had been tormented by the afflictions arising from the concepts of ‘I’ and ‘mine,’ has finally been freed from the sorrows of its entanglement with material nature.”

*   **You are the One Form:** You possess the causal form, being one with the subtle sentient and insentient universe before creation, as described in the scripture, “It was one, indeed.”
*   **the two forms:** You have as Your forms the great principle of intellect and the principle of "I-ness" (the effects that emerge from that unmanifest cause).
*   **the three forms:** You have as Your modes the varying expressions of the three universal qualities—goodness, passion, and darkness—which arise from the threefold nature of the great principle and the principle of "I-ness."
*   **becoming the many forms:** You have as Your modes the eleven senses (the effects of the principle of "I-ness" in its mode of goodness).
*   **the five elements:** You have as Your modes the five great elements (the effects of the principle of "I-ness" in its mode of darkness).
*   **the two luminaries:** You have as Your form the two luminaries, the sun and moon, which are cited as examples of the creation of individual entities within the cosmic egg.
*   **and the formless Spirit:** You become the subtle one by entering into all these entities as their inner Self.
*   **(For the protection of the created beings, You)** **mounted the serpent:** You ascended Your couch, the divine Adisesa.
*   **and slumbered in the middle of the ocean:** You gracefully reclined in the midst of the Milk Ocean.
*   **O Narayana!:** O You who are the true subject of the name "Narayana," a status established by this inner pervasion of all beings and Your reclining on the ocean!
*   **(By the process described above), embracing Your entire being**—Your form and Your breathtaking beauty, wherein You accept all my faculties as Your complete adornment—**within my heart, my soul has been freed from the anguish** it suffered in the previous Tiruvaymoli decad.

Some commentators interpret the sequence up to “the many forms” as referring to the supreme (Para), emanational (Vyuha), and incarnate (Vibhava) forms. In this view:
*   “One Form” refers to the supreme form of Vasudeva, which is preeminent and the ultimate goal.
*   “two forms” refers to its two states: that which is manifest to perfected sages and that which remains unmanifest.
*   “three forms” refers to the unmanifest state, which is threefold, comprising Sankarshana, Pradyumna, and Aniruddha.
*   “many forms” refers to their expansions into the twelve secondary emanations like Govinda and the ten primary divine descents.
*   The phrase “the five elements, the two luminaries” is then said to refer to His connection with the universe of His divine play (līlā-vibhūti).

**இ – (24000 Commentary):**

In the third verse, the Alvar speaks of how the Lord Himself has attained fulfillment through this loving union.

[You are the One Form] This describes the immense effort the Lord undertook to bring the Alvar to this state. It refers to that state before creation when “these things” (the universe) did not exist, yet He existed, and by His mere existence, He sustained their potential existence. Alternatively, it can refer to His unique, transcendent form that exists prior to creation.

[the two forms] This is the state where, desiring to create, He takes primal matter and the great principle of intellect as His body.

[the three forms, and so on] He is the one who has as His body the threefold principle of “I-ness,” the eleven senses that are the effect of this principle in its mode of goodness, and the elements like earth that are the effect of this principle in its mode of darkness.

[the two luminaries] As stated in the scriptures, "The Creator fashioned the sun and the moon as before," the moon and sun are representative of the entire class of created effects.

[and the formless Spirit] As the scripture says, “Having created it, He then entered into it,” this refers to His becoming the inner Self of all created things for the sake of their sustenance. If this is said of insentient matter, what about the soul? Since the scripture also states, “Entering with this soul-self,” His entry as the inner Self of the individual soul is also included.

[who mounted the serpent, and so on] You are the one who reclined upon the divine Adisesa in the middle of the Milk Ocean, so that created beings like Brahma could have a Lord to take refuge in!

[O Narayana!] This name speaks of the inherent relationship You have with all beings, which is the foundation for Your being equally compassionate at all times.

[Embracing Your entire being within my heart] “Ākam” means body. All that Your divine form requires—sandalwood paste, garlands, fine robes, and radiant ornaments—You have caused them all to manifest within my heart. This, indeed, is the true purpose of creation.

But who became free from sorrow by this act? He did.

[the soul’s anguish has been destroyed] The anguish that was in *Your* divine heart has finally been vanquished. The Alvar says to Him, “Having managed to bring this soul safely to the shore, You have at last fulfilled Your purpose!” Alternatively, this line can also mean, “My own soul has become free from sorrow.”

There are also those who interpret this verse as describing the supreme (Para), emanational (Vyuha), and incarnate (Vibhava) forms. In this view, “One Form” is the supreme state; “two forms” refers to the Vyuha, mentioning Vasudeva and Sankarshana; “three forms” refers to Pradyumna; and “many forms” refers to the divine descents. The lines “the five elements, the two luminaries” would then refer to the cosmic creation for which certain emanations are responsible, and “reclining on the ocean of milk” would refer to the divine protection for which other emanations are responsible.

**ஈடு: – (36000 Commentary):**

In this third verse, the Alvar declares to the Lord, “You have made this soul Your own and have, by that very act, become utterly fulfilled.”

[You are the One Form] As the scripture states, “Existence alone, my dear, was this in the beginning” (Chandogya Upanishad 6.2.1), this verse describes the state prior to creation. The entire universe, having been dissolved, lay indistinct, its potential held within Him like the power dormant in ash-covered embers. He alone existed. This speaks of the state where He is poised for creation. (Alternatively, it can also refer to His unique, transcendent form that exists before creation.)

[the two forms] This describes the state where He gazes upon unmanifest primal matter (Prakṛti) and the great principle of intellect (Mahat), taking them as His divine body. It describes Him as possessing both cause and effect as His body. They are mentioned together because of their proximity; the manifest state of the unmanifest is Mahat.

[the three forms] This describes the state where He has the threefold principle of “I-ness” (Ahaṅkāra) as His body, which is divided into the modes of goodness (Sāttvika), passion (Rājasa), and darkness (Tāmasa).

[becoming the many forms, the five elements] The order mentioned here is not the linear sequence of creation described earlier. Rather, it speaks of Him as having as His body:
*   the effects of the principle of “I-ness” in its mode of goodness: the eleven senses.
*   the effects of the principle of “I-ness” in its mode of darkness: the five great elements, endowed with their qualities.
The principle of “I-ness” in its mode of passion serves as an auxiliary to the other two. Thus, the verse says He has the eleven senses and the five elements as His divine body.

[the two luminaries] As the scripture states, “The Creator fashioned the sun and the moon as before,” the sun and moon are representative of the entire class of individually created effects.

[and the formless Spirit] Having created all these, as the scripture says, “Having created it, He then entered into it. Having entered into it, He became both the manifest and the unmanifest” (Taittiriya Upanishad 2.6.1), He enters them to grant them existence and a name. This describes the state where He abides within them as their inner Self. If this refers to His entry into insentient matter, does this not also apply to the soul? Indeed it does, for the scripture says, “Entering with this soul-self…” (Chandogya Upanishad 6.3.2), indicating that His entry is as the inner Self of the individual soul as well.

[who mounted the serpent] So that created beings like Brahma could have a Lord to take refuge in, He reclined in the middle of the Milk Ocean on the divine Adisesa. Just as a farmer, after planting his crop, builds a small hut at the edge of the field to watch over it, so the Lord, having created these beings, reclines nearby to ensure their protection.

[O Narayana!] This name speaks of the inherent relationship He has with all beings, a relationship that is the very reason for His being uniformly compassionate at all times.

[Embracing Your entire being within my heart] “Ākam” means body. All that Your divine form requires—sandalwood paste, garlands, silken robes, and radiant ornaments—You made them all arise from within my heart. This, after all, is the true purpose of creation.

A question arises: Who became free from sorrow by this act? He did.

[the soul’s anguish has been destroyed] The anguish that was in *Your* divine heart has at last been vanquished. The Alvar says to Him, “Having finally brought this soul safely to the far shore, You have fulfilled Your great purpose!” Alternatively, the line can mean, “My own soul has become free from sorrow.” However, since this entire decad is about the Lord’s profound love for the devotee, the interpretation that it is the Lord’s sorrow that has been relieved is the most fitting.

There are also those who interpret this verse as referring to the Para, Vyuha, and Vibhava forms. They see “One Form” as Para Vasudeva; “two forms” as Vyuha Vasudeva and Sankarshana; “three forms” as Pradyumna; and “many forms” as the divine descents. “The five elements” then refers to the act of creation, a duty of Pradyumna, while “reclining on the serpent” refers to Aniruddha and His duty of protection. The act of dissolution, a duty of Sankarshana, is implied, since creation is always preceded by dissolution. But this interpretation is not as natural as the first.
