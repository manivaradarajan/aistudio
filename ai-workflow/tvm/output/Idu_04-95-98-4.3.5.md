Verse 5.8.5

**Verse Translation**

For Kannan, my Master, my great Benefactor, the wielder of the discus of Time—
My very soul is His garland.
My love is His radiant golden crown and His countless ornaments.
That same love is His befitting robe and the fame the three worlds draw near to acclaim.

**ஆ – (6000 Commentary):**

For Kannan, who wields the divine discus whose very nature is to consume His foes, the sacred Tulasi garland meant to adorn His holy feet, chest, and shoulders is my own soul. He says: My love is His innumerable and varied divine ornaments, His befitting divine yellow silk garment, the fame extolled by all the worlds, and everything else.

**ஒ - (9000 Commentary):**

In the fifth verse, he says that each of his loving affections has become all of the Lord’s ornaments and other accoutrements.

[My love…] My love alone is His extraordinary sacred crown, His countless ornaments, the beautiful silk robe that befits His waist, and the fame He cherishes and is praised for. All this is for Krishna, my Lord—the one who holds in His sacred hand the divine discus that governs Time, who by revealing its beauty performed the great favor of making me his own, and who is my Master.

**ப – (12000 Commentary):**

Next, the Alvar says: For Krishna—who is delightful, who is my Lord, my benefactor, and easily accessible—my love and other qualities have become all of His ornaments and attire.

To Kannan (Krishna, who made Himself accessible by incarnating in tune with the times), who is the wielder of the discus that governs Time; who is my Master who enslaved me (by revealing that beauty and saving me from subjugation to time); and who is my great Benefactor (who grants me the experience of that beauty for as long as time exists)—my soul, the very substance of my self, has become His garland. My love, the inherent quality of my soul, has become His ornaments, beginning with His golden, radiant crown, countless ornaments which are made of gold and possess a brilliance indicating His supreme sovereignty. That same love is His befitting robe, the sacred yellow silk garment appropriate for Him (and indicative of His supremacy over all). That same love is the fame which the three worlds draw near to proclaim with tireless praise. The core meaning is that the Lord considers His becoming the object of the Alvar’s love to be His greatest adornment and His highest glory.

**இ - (24000 Commentary):**

In the fifth verse, he says: Each of my loving affections has become everything for Him, from His ornaments to all else!

[My very soul is His garland.] You see, it is the very thing I consider ‘mine’ that He takes as His garland. It would be fitting for Him to accept as His garland the one known as “the garland on His chest” [Sri Piratti]. Instead of her, He takes my very existence as His garland.

[My love is His radiant golden crown...] My love is His desirable crown that indicates supreme sovereignty and His countless, varied sacred ornaments. The Lord considers the great fortune of becoming the object of the Alvar's love to be equal to being adorned with all His ornaments.

[That same love is His befitting robe.] My love is also the sacred yellow silk garment, the sign of the Supreme Person, perfectly suited for His divine waist. As the Alvar says, "Even in my unknowing youth, He fostered love in me for servitude," his love is inherent to his very existence. There is the story of the Srivaishnava washerman who starched the sacred garments. As this is a “song free from falsehood,” one need not wonder, ‘Is this possible?’ The scripture too says, “His garment is of a beautiful saffron hue” (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 2.3.6 [?]).

[That same love is the fame the three worlds draw near to acclaim.] My love is also His fame, which all beings approach and praise devotedly. It is not something that one person knows while another does not; hence, everyone praises Him with one voice. He considers the praise offered by the Alvar to be equivalent to the praise offered by all beings. The verse proceeds to describe how this love was cultivated.

[For Kannan, my Master, my great Benefactor, the wielder of the discus of Time.] For Krishna, my Lord, who showed me the beauty of His hand and discus, performed the great favor of making me devoted to nothing else, and brought me to the state of constant devotion. As the Alvar says elsewhere, “Our Kannan, of a form like a dark raincloud, cultivated a love surpassing even the great love of the sages, vast as the ocean.”

[The discus of Time.] This is the discus that governs Time, or the discus that frees one from subjugation to time. Even in an age when people “will not worship Him,” this discus, by its union with His sacred hand, inspires loving taste.

**ஈடு: – (36000 Commentary):**

**Fifth Verse.** The Alvar says that each of his loving affections has become all of the Lord’s ornaments and other accoutrements.

[My very soul is His garland.] You see, it is this thing I consider ‘mine’ that He has taken as His garland. It would be fitting for Him to take as His garland the one who is called “the garland on His chest” [Sri Piratti]. Instead of her, you see, He takes my very existence as His garland.

[My love is His radiant golden crown and all His countless ornaments.] My love is His desirable crown that indicates supreme sovereignty and His countless, varied sacred ornaments. If you ask, “How can the Alvar’s love become an ornament?” It is said in this way because the Lord considers the very fact of His becoming the object of the Alvar’s love to be as glorious as wearing countless ornaments.

[That same love is His befitting robe.] His sacred yellow silk garment, perfectly suited for His divine waist, is also that same love. One day, a Srivaishnava washerman brought the Lord’s sacred garments, beautifully prepared, and showed them to Emperumanar [Ramanuja]. Greatly pleased, Emperumanar took him by the hand, entered the Lord's presence, and said, “My Lord! You must cast Your divine eyes upon the way he has prepared these garments to be worthy of Your divine waist!” The Lord, delighted, graced Udayavar with His blessings and declared, “For this man’s sake, We have forgiven the offense the washerman [of Kamsa] committed against Us in a previous age.”

[That same love is His befitting robe.] This love has ceased being just many things of a single kind (ornaments) and has begun to become many things of different kinds. That same love is His sacred yellow silk garment, the very sign of the Supreme Person. After all, the sacred ornaments and sacred garment are what the Alvar has been fashioning over a long time. As he says, "Even in my unknowing youth, He fostered love in me for servitude," this love of his is inherent to his very existence. Scripture states, “His garment is of a beautiful saffron hue” (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 2.3.6 [?]). Since this is a “song free from falsehood,” one need not doubt whether this is possible.

[That same love is the fame the three worlds draw near to acclaim.] That love is also the fame praised not just by the wise, but by all beings in the three worlds who draw near and praise Him like a roaring ocean. The Lord thinks, “After this one began to love Me, even those devoted to other ends have become solely devoted to Me and praise Me.”

The following lines describe how He Himself cultivated this love. It seems it was by showing the Alvar the circular discus that He cultivated this deep disposition in him.

[For Kannan, my Master, my great Benefactor, the wielder of the discus of Time.] For Krishna, my Lord, who showed me the beauty of His hand and discus, performed the great favor of making me devoted to nothing else, and brought me to the state described as: “Is there sleep for the eyes of those who have attained a love that makes time fly?” (Tiruviruttam 97).

[The discus of Time.] He is, after all, the one capable of turning day into night. This is the discus that directs time, or it is the discus that removes one’s subjugation to time. Even in an age when people “will not worship Him,” in this world of great darkness, this discus dispels the darkness and, like the sacred feet which it never leaves just as it never leaves His hand, becomes a source of loving devotion—the “grace-filled, radiant discus.” Standing in His hand, it has the power to make this devotee fall at His feet.
