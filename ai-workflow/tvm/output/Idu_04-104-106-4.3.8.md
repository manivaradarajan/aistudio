**Verse Translation**

O You who cherish my very soul!
O luminous Lord whose form is pure knowing,
You stand pervading the seven beautiful worlds,
permeating them completely, becoming all that is!
My soul has become Yours, and Your soul, mine.
You stood in just this way—
but how could I ever find words to praise You?

**ஆ – (6000 Commentary):**

O Lord, as the inner controller, You entered all worlds! Though Your every desire is already fulfilled, You stood as my sole sustainer. You mingled with me so completely that my soul became subject to Your will, and Your own divine, essential nature became subject to my will. Having described the way You united with him, the Alvar feels that even this is not enough. He concludes that the union is beyond the realm of words, for he can say nothing more about *how* You mingled with me.

**ஒ - (9000 Commentary):**

In this eighth verse, the Alvar declares that the union that blossomed between him and the Lord—a union in which their very essence, existence, and actions became mutually dependent—is beyond the realm of speech.

[Starting with “O You who cherish my very soul”] "To unite with me, You became all-pervading so that I could not escape You, O Lord whose very nature is consciousness!" Alternatively, this may be read as: "O Lord, You possess a dominion renewed by the supreme joy born of Your union with me! Within that dominion, You pervade everything fully, O You whose very nature is consciousness!"

[Starting with “My soul has become Yours”] My soul is now subject to Your will, and Your divine, essential self has become subject to my will.

[You stood in just this way—but how could I ever find words to praise You?] The way You Yourself came seeking me and now stand before me is beyond the realm of description.

**ப – (12000 Commentary):**

Next, the Alvar asks, "Is it possible to describe the way You pervaded all the worlds for my sake and accepted me?"

**O You who cherish my very soul** – O One who, being deeply attached to the substance of my soul,

**(and to Your subservient domains,)** **the seven beautiful worlds** – in all the seven kinds of worlds, which are inherently splendid,

**permeating them completely, becoming all that is** – pervaded them fully, standing in such a way that all substances are Your modes,

**O luminous Lord whose form is pure knowing** – O You whose essential nature is self-luminous knowledge!

**My soul has become Yours, and Your soul, mine** – My essential soul-nature has become an object of Your enjoyment, and Your divine, essential self has become an object of my enjoyment.

**You stood in just this way** – You stood in this very manner.

**but how could I ever find words to praise You?** – What song could I possibly frame to describe this? Thus, the mutual love of both is expressed.

**இ - (24000 Commentary)**

In the eighth verse, the Alvar declares that the union that has blossomed between them, where one cannot be described without the other, is beyond the realm of speech.

[O You who cherish my very soul] This can be understood in two ways. First, "Because of His intense desire to attain me, He became all-pervading, just as one who wishes to capture a person lays siege to the entire town." Or, it may mean, "Because of the great love He felt upon attaining the Alvar, even His all-pervasiveness gained a new fullness." By attaining the Alvar, both of His divine realms were renewed.

[permeating them completely, becoming all that is] This is His state of pervading everywhere densely, without any gap, just as a universal quality is fully present in every individual instance of that class. O You whose essential nature is the most expansive knowledge! My soul became subject to Your will, and Your own divine, essential self became subject to my will.

Someone may ask, "How can the Independent Lord be subject to another's will?" In response, Āḷavantār taught, "If it can happen, it happens. This is not a matter for rational analysis." When sin overwhelms him, this soul is one who says, "I am not Yours." But when the Lord decides to make Himself belong to someone, there are no impediments; that alone stands firm.

[You stood in just this way] You stood in exactly this manner.

[but how could I ever find words to praise You?] Is this a state that I can capture in song and speak of?

**ஈடு: – (36000 Commentary):**

**Eighth Verse.** The Alvar declares that the union which has blossomed between them—a union so profound that one cannot exist without the other—is beyond the realm of speech.

[O You who cherish my very soul] This may be interpreted in two ways: “O You who possess the supreme joy that came from attaining my very existence!” or “O You who possess the intense desire to attain me!” It may also be taken as a direct address.

[You stand pervading the seven beautiful worlds] Corresponding to the previous lines, this has two meanings. It could mean that His all-pervasiveness itself was renewed by the joy of attaining the Alvar. Or, the Alvar may feel that the Lord became all-pervading specifically to capture him, just as one lays siege to a whole town out of an intense desire for a single person within it.

[the seven beautiful worlds] This refers to the new splendor that arose for the entire cosmos after the Lord came and mingled with the Alvar. Just as it is said, “A good meal makes the body glow,” when the soul of the cosmos (the Lord) is fulfilled, its body (the cosmos) is also renewed. For He is the One for whom the cosmos is the body.

[permeating them completely, becoming all that is] This describes how He pervades everything completely, without any gaps, just as a universal quality is wholly present in every individual instance of its kind.

[O luminous Lord whose form is pure knowing] O You whose essential nature is luminous knowledge!

[My soul has become Yours, and Your soul, mine] “My soul is Yours, and Your soul is mine.” My essential nature is subject to Your will, and Your essential nature is subject to my will.

When Āḷavantār was explaining this passage, some of the disciples present asked, “We can understand how this soul’s nature is subject to His will, but what does it mean for *His* nature to be subject to this soul’s will?”

Āḷavantār replied, “On the day this soul decides, ‘I will make myself His,’ his past karma can still stand in the way. But on the day the Supreme Lord decides to make Himself subject to this soul’s will, there are no impediments. Therefore, it is *that* which stands firm. In fact,” he continued, “the real doubt should be whether *this soul's* nature can truly become subject to His will!”

[You stood in just this way] You stood in this very manner.

[but how could I ever find words to praise You?] The Alvar concludes, “Was it ever possible for me to frame Your magnificent quality of loving affection in a song and speak of it?”
