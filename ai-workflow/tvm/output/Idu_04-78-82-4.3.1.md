**Verse 4.3.1**

For the sake of the maiden with lips like scarlet *kōvai* fruit, You shattered the necks of the bulls!
You bent Your bow to make the lord of walled Lanka fall!
You broke the tusk of the noble, fine elephant of high lineage!
Though I did not worship You at those times, strewing flowers in sacred water,
for Your divine body, which is itself like a dark *pūvai* flower, the soothing sandal paste is my very heart.

**ஆ – (6000 Commentary):**

“O Lord, You eliminated the enemies of those dear to You, such as the seven powerful bull-shaped asuras, the ten-headed Ravana, and the elephant Kuvalayapida. Though I failed to perform the customary mixed services at those times to soothe the exertion born of those deeds, my own mind has become the divine unguent for Your sacred body—a body that is cool, supremely fragrant, and as delicate as a smiling flower. My mind, a fitting unguent, soothes all Your exertion. What wondrous loving intimacy is this?” says the Alvar.

**ஒ - (9000 Commentary):**

In the first verse, the Alvar says, “Even though I was not able to perform the mixed services that were needed to relieve the fatigue that came from Your destroying the enemies of Your devotees, the very existence of my heart has become that mixed service for You.”

[For the sake of the maiden with lips like scarlet *kōvai* fruit...] Vanquished by the smile of Nappinnai Piratti, You broke the mighty necks of the bulls for the sake of uniting with her. You graciously bent Your bow so that Ravana, the unworthy lord of Lanka, would perish. You effortlessly broke the tusk of the great elephant, which, being born of a noble lineage and possessing all perfect features, was impossible for anyone to even assess. [Strewing flowers...] Even though I was not able to perform service for You with water and flowers at the proper times. [For Your divine body, which is itself like a dark *pūvai* flower] This can also be read as, “Strewing flowers…” For Your body which has the nature of the *pūvai* flower. Alternatively, it means, for Your body that can never be without flowers.

**ப – (12000 Commentary):**

In the first verse, addressing the Supreme Lord, who by His very nature destroys the obstacles to attainment, the Alvar says, “You have taken my heart as a sacred unguent for Yourself.”

**For the sake of the maiden with lips** red like the *kōvai* fruit—Nappinnai Piratti—**You shattered the necks of the bulls.** To make Ravana, the **lord of walled Lanka, fall**, **You bent Your bow.** You effortlessly **broke the tusk of the noble** (excellence of features), **fine** (excellence of breed) **elephant**—Kuvalayapida. (Though You did all this to remove the obstacles for Nappinnai, for the daughter of King Janaka, and for the maidens of Mathura,) **even though I did not worship You at the proper times, strewing water that does not leave flowers** behind (i.e., serving out of love in a beautiful, disorderly manner) **and bowing down.** For **Your divine body, which is like a dark *pūvai* flower** in its hue, **the soothing sandal paste** fit **to be smeared is my very heart.**

The inner meaning is: You ignored the flaws of the vessel—my heart—and taking my love alone as Your support, You made it into a sacred unguent for Yourself. The reason for this is that He is the one who dispels the unblossomed state of concealment, destroys the mental anguish born of dualities and subjugation to the senses, and annihilates the wicked pride that obstructs one’s entry to Him.

**இ - (24000 Commentary):**

In the first verse, the Alvar says, “When You were destroying the enemies of Your devotees, I was not even there to show my face and be of service to You. How is it that You have taken my heart as Your object of enjoyment?” Alternatively, he says, “While Nappinnai Piratti and the daughter of King Janaka are Your beloveds, how is it that You treat my heart as if it were everything to You?”

[For the sake of the maiden with lips like scarlet *kōvai* fruit] Before Krishna’s arrival, Nappinnai was sullen, thinking, “What would I do if another hero wins?” But as soon as Krishna appeared, she smiled. Seeing that, He felt it was a sight worth dying for. [You shattered the necks of the bulls] Vanquished by that smile, He threw Himself upon the bulls without any concern for His own safety. “Necks” refers to the nape of their necks, which was the very source of their pride; He broke it. His deed was like a warrior who, with his bare hands, goes and snatches the weapon from his opponent’s grasp. At that time, I was not there to say, “These are not mere bulls; they are possessed by asuras,” just as Lakshmana warned Rama about the magical deer when He was about to pursue it.

[Walled Lanka] Ravana committed his transgressions thinking, “I have this city and these ramparts to protect me.” He did not understand that the protective power of a fortress comes from the will of the one who comes to destroy it. He took the Supreme Protector as his enemy and considered the walls his protection. This is the kind of “protection” a soul arranges for itself. [To make the lord of Lanka fall, You bent Your bow] So that Ravana, the controller of Lanka, would perish. Just as the Rakshasas know only warfare of illusion, He knows only direct, righteous battle. I was not there, like Vibhishana, to inform Him, “Just as you are skilled in righteous thought, the Rakshasas are skilled in employing illusion.”

[You broke the tusk of the noble, fine elephant] You effortlessly broke the tusk of Kuvalayapida, an elephant born of a great lineage and endowed with all perfect features, making it impossible for anyone to estimate its worth. At that time, I was not there to cry out, “This is not a fair fight!”

[Though I did not worship You at those times, strewing water that does not leave flowers behind] This means either strewing flowers and sprinkling water at those respective times, or sprinkling water that does not leave flowers—that is, water mixed with flowers. When a loving devotee simply bows at His feet, all His fatigue vanishes. Even though I was not able to perform such mixed services at those times…

[For Your divine body, which is like a dark *pūvai* flower] For the body that can never be without flowers. Or, for the body that *is* the *pūvai* flower. “*Pūvai vī*” means the *pūvai* flower, which has the nature of the dark *kāyā* blossom. For His divine body, as delicate as a smiling flower. [The soothing sandal paste to be smeared… is my very heart] For that beautiful form, the sandal paste to be applied has become my heart! For Him who knows the inner and outer qualities of sandal paste, my heart has become the unguent to be smeared on Him!

**ஈடு: – (36000 Commentary):**

In the first verse, the Alvar says: “While Nappinnai Piratti, the daughter of King Janaka, and others are Your cherished beloveds, You show the same disposition toward me that You show them, making my very existence everything to You.” Alternatively, he says: “During the times You destroyed the enemies of Your devotees, I was not present to show my face and render service. This being the case, why do You take my heart as Your object of enjoyment?”

[For the sake of the maiden with lips like scarlet *kōvai* fruit] This phrase is meant to describe the beauty of her form, which was so captivating that it made Krishna throw Himself upon those bulls without any thought for His own safety. They adorned Nappinnai and brought her forward, announcing, “We will give her in marriage to whoever can subdue these bulls.” Seeing her, Krishna thought, “If I can embrace her, is it not worth breaking these bulls?” and threw Himself upon them, heedless of Himself. She had been sullen, worried, “What if Nambi Muttapiran (Balarama) arrives first and victory falls to him?” But as soon as Krishna appeared, she smiled out of overwhelming joy. The verse describes the deep crimson of her lips at that moment. Vanquished by that smile, He made Himself entirely hers.

[You shattered the necks of the bulls] He broke the necks of the bulls. “Necks” refers to the nape, the very source of their pride, and He broke it. What He did was like a hero who walks up and snatches a weapon from his enemy’s hand barehanded. After all, the bulls stood with their ultimate weapons—their horns—ready. It was my duty to be there at that moment and say, “These have been possessed by asuras!” just as Lakshmana, with perfect clarity, stood and declared, “This is a magical deer, a Rakshasa illusion!” when Rama was setting out in pursuit. But I failed to do that, says the Alvar.

[Walled Lanka] It was because he thought, “I have this fortress and this city to protect me,” that Ravana dared to oppose the son of the Emperor. He forsook the Universal Protector, Rama, and believed the walls were his protection. This is the kind of “protection” a soul arranges for itself.

[To make the lord of Lanka fall] As it is said, “Lanka, ruled by Ravana” (Ramayana, Sundara Kanda 1.8 [?]). The land itself is nearly impossible for anyone to enter, and on top of that, there is the strength of the one who rules from within. So that Ravana, who was filled with the pride, “Am I not the ruler of fortified Lanka?” would perish.

[You bent Your bow] Just as the Rakshasas know nothing but the warfare of illusion, He knows nothing but direct, righteous battle. I was not there like Vibhishana to inform him, “Just as you are skilled in righteous warfare, the Rakshasas, sir, are skilled in employing illusion.”

[You broke the tusk of the noble, fine elephant] You effortlessly broke the tusk of Kuvalayapida, an elephant born of a great lineage and endowed with all auspicious features. At that moment, I was not there among the women of Mathura to cry out, “They say this is not a fair fight!”

[Strewing water that does not leave flowers behind] This can mean offering flowers at His sacred feet and then sprinkling water. Or, it can mean water from which flowers are never separate—that is, water mixed with flowers—and sprinkling that.

[At those times] Even though, at those respective times—when You subdued the seven bulls and so on—I did not perform the mixed services needed to relieve the fatigue born of those deeds…

[For Your divine body, which is like a dark *pūvai* flower] For the divine body that cannot be without flowers. Alternatively, for the body that is like the *pūvai* flower itself, for “*vī*” is a name for a flower. For His body, which is as delicate as a smiling flower.

[The soothing sandal paste to be smeared on Your divine body] As it is said, “Suitable for our bodies” (Vishnu Purana 5.20.10 [?]). When Balarama and Krishna entered Mathura, they saw the hunchbacked woman and asked, “Can you give us some sandal paste, good enough to be applied on my elder brother and me?”
*   She gave them the ordinary paste meant for common use.
*   He said, “This smells wonderful!” indicating it was for Him, but then she showed him a higher grade.
*   He said, “This is fit for a king,” meaning it was for Kamsa.
*   She then showed him an even higher grade.
*   He said, “This is fit for a hero,” meaning its color alone could be enjoyed.

When He spoke thus, she smiled, her joy evident. Krishna, thinking, “How could these two, accustomed to the smell of butter, know the distinctions in sandal paste?” then exclaimed, “What a beautiful face this is, now that she is offering fine unguent!” He continued, “Those who trade in fine goods must know the difference in the bodies of those who will use them. Give us the sandal paste that is worthy of my body and my brother’s body.”

For the divine body that He anoints with such exquisite discernment, my heart has become the soothing unguent
