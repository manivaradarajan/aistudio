**Verse 4.3.10**

Even if I praise Him,
even if all seven worlds praise Him,
and even if He Himself praises Himself,
praising and praising Him again and again, where could such praise ever reach an end?
Yet, because He sweetens my soul like honey, milk, raw sugar, and nectar,
I praised my Lord—I praised Him, simply to survive.

**ஆ – (6000 Commentary):**

If it is asked, “Did you praise Him because you were capable of doing so?” the Āḻvār replies:
Even if I, all souls residing in all worlds, and the Lord Himself—who is distinguished by His natural qualities of omniscience and so on—were to stand together and praise Him for all time, could we even approach the shore of His limitless ocean of fame?

If it is then asked, “Why then do you attempt to praise His fame, which even the Lord Himself cannot exhaustively praise?” he replies:
I praised Him simply because He was so sweet.

If it is further asked, “But would anyone undertake something impossible just because it is sweet?” he concludes:
I praised Him because I could not sustain myself otherwise.

**ஒ – (9000 Commentary):**

In this tenth verse, the Āḻvār says, “I praised Him for my own salvation, for I could not let go of Him, who is distinguished by the quality of His overwhelming love—a love that cannot be fully praised by me, by all the worlds, or even by the Supreme Lord Himself—due to the sheer abundance of His relishability.”

**ப – (12000 Commentary):**

Next, the Āḻvār says, “I praised the One whose immeasurable greatness cannot be fathomed by anyone, solely for the sake of my own existence.”

Even if **I** (who was granted delusion-free, true knowledge by Him) **praise Him**, and all **seven worlds praise Him completely**, without distinction between the ignorant and the all-knowing; and **even if, beyond that, He Himself** (who is omniscient and the giver of knowledge to all) **praises Himself** (whose essential nature is boundless), **praising and praising Him again and again** (without ever repeating what was once said), **where** will it **reach an end**? (Nevertheless, the reason for praising Him is that) He, becoming like **honey, milk, raw sugar, and nectar**, **sweetens** my soul in every way. **My Lord Himself**, the great benefactor who made me experience this, **I praised**. For my very **survival, I, even I, praised Him**.

**இ – (24000 Commentary):**

In this tenth verse, the Āḻvār says, “I praised the one who cannot be fully praised even if everyone—without distinctions of their nature, without dispute between the ruler and the ruled—praises Him with a single voice. I did so out of my own powerlessness to do otherwise.”

“[Even if] I praise Him…” refers to himself, who was granted delusion-free, true knowledge. He praises Him along with all others, with no distinction between the ignorant who are devoted to other things and those of superior knowledge who are devoted to Him alone. “…even if He Himself praises…” He who is the very source of knowledge for all other beings, who is innately omniscient, and who is the all-powerful Lord capable of completing any task He begins—even if He praises Himself.

“[praises] Himself again and again, where could such praise ever reach an end?” The portion that is praised will always be finite, while the unpraised portion will remain overwhelmingly vast. Where can one possibly reach the end?

One might ask, “Knowing that He is so boundless, why did you even venture into this?” The Āḻvār replies: “I plunged into it because of His relishability, as scripture says, ‘He is all tastes’ (Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.12 [?]), and from the memory of the help He rendered by bestowing His magnificent quality of love upon me.”

But can this be compared to a rational act? Just because one delights in seeing the grand temple food offering, would anyone, forgetting the limits of their own stomach, dare to try and measure it all? No. But then, does a person act with careful discrimination when a ruffian suddenly attacks them? My praise was like that.

“[I praised Him] simply to survive.” I, who longed to see His glorious deeds that are far removed in time, praised Him for my own survival, not for the sake of measuring or defining Him.

**ஈடு: – (36000 Commentary):**

In this tenth verse, the Āḻvār declares, “I praised the One who cannot be fully praised even if I, all the worlds, and the Supreme Lord Himself praise Him with a single voice. I did so out of my own helplessness.”

“[Even if] I praise Him”: I, who was granted delusion-free, true knowledge by Him, praise Him.

“[even if] all seven worlds praise Him completely”: All beings praise Him, without any distinction between the specially knowledgeable and the unknowledgeable, making no difference between “the small” and “the great.”

“[and even if, beyond that,] He Himself praises”: He, who is innately omniscient and possesses all the power needed to successfully complete any task He begins, praises Himself. Even if everyone praises Him with a single voice in this way, the part that is praised remains finite, while the part yet to be praised remains immense. When it comes to the inability to fathom this Subject, there is no difference between those devoted to other things and those devoted solely to Him, between the finite and the infinite.

If so, why did you venture to praise Him?
The Āḻvār answers, “[Because He sweetens my soul like honey…]” I praised Him for two reasons:
*   First, because of the sheer relishability of this Subject, who is described in scripture as “He is all fragrances, He is all tastes” (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 3.14.2).
*   Second, because He is the great benefactor who bestowed His quality of love in such a way that it compelled me to think, “I *must* plunge into this Subject and praise Him!”

One might object, “Should you, a soul without flaw, venture into something so boundless merely because He is sweet and a benefactor?”

“[I praised Him] simply to survive.” After all, is it not true that only those who cannot live without praising Him will praise Him?
